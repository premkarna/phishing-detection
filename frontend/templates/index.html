How to Use This Platform: Learning Path

New to phishing detection? Start with basic features and progress to advanced threat hunting as you gain experience. Each level builds on the previous one.

Step 1: Basic

Getting Started

  • Simple URL Scan: Paste any link and get instant safe/dangerous result with risk percentage (0-100%)
  • QR Code Reading: Upload QR images to see decoded content and basic safety check
  • Read AI Verdict: Understand Gemini's explanation of why something is safe or phishing
  • View Screenshots: See webpage previews without visiting dangerous sites

You are: End user, student, or non-technical staff

Step 2: Intermediate

Security Analysis

  • URL Tracing: Follow redirect chains through bit.ly, tinyurl to find real destination
  • WHOIS Lookup: Check domain age, owner, SSL certificate validity
  • Email Headers: Inspect SPF, DKIM, DMARC authentication markers
  • Sandbox View: Watch what happens when malicious URL opens

You are: SOC analyst or incident responder

Step 3: Advanced

Threat Hunting

  • QR Fingerprinting: Track QR codes across multiple campaigns and time periods
  • Attribution: Match attacks to known threat actors like TA-QR-002
  • Honeytokens: Deploy bait credentials and detect when attackers use them
  • SIEM Integration: Auto-push detections to Splunk with full context

You are: Threat intel researcher or security engineer

Pro Tip: Don't worry if you don't understand all features at once. Start with Basic (Step 1), scan a few URLs/QRs, then gradually explore more advanced features as you become comfortable.

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack where adversaries impersonate trusted entities — banks, tech companies, colleagues — to steal credentials, financial data, or install malware. It exploits human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities, making awareness your strongest defense.

Common Attack Vectors

Email Phishing

Bulk fraudulent emails mimicking legitimate senders to harvest credentials or deliver malware via malicious links and attachments.

Spoofed Sender Malicious Links Fake Attachments
Smishing (SMS)

SMS-based phishing using urgent messages about package deliveries, bank alerts, or prize notifications to lure victims to fake sites.

Fake SMS Urgency Tactics
Vishing (Voice)

Phone-based social engineering where attackers impersonate banks, government agencies, or IT support to extract sensitive info.

Caller ID Spoof Social Engineering
Quishing (QR Code)

Malicious QR codes embedded in emails, flyers, or stickers that redirect victims to credential-harvesting websites.

Fake QR URL Redirect
Clone Phishing

Attackers duplicate a legitimate email previously received by the target, replacing links or attachments with malicious versions.

Replicated Email Swapped Payload
Social Engineering

Psychological manipulation — pretexting, baiting, tailgating — that bypasses technical controls by exploiting human trust.

Pretexting Baiting Impersonation

Red Flags to Watch

Urgency & Pressure

"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" — attackers create panic to bypass rational thinking.

Mismatched URLs

Hover over links before clicking — displayed text may say "paypal.com" but actually points to "paypa1-secure.ru".

Generic Greetings

"Dear Customer" instead of your actual name signals a mass phishing campaign.

Unexpected Requests

Legitimate organizations never ask for passwords, OTPs, or credit card numbers via email or SMS.

Grammar & Spelling Errors

Poor language quality is often a giveaway — though AI-generated phishing is increasingly polished.

Suspicious Attachments

Unexpected .exe, .zip, .docx with macros, or .pdf files can contain malware or exploits.

Protection Best Practices

Enable MFA

Multi-Factor Authentication stops attackers even if your password is stolen.

Verify Senders

Always check the actual email address, not just the display name. Contact via official channels to confirm.

Keep Systems Updated

Patches close known vulnerabilities that phishing payloads exploit to gain initial access.

How to Report Phishing

Email: Forward the suspicious email to reportphishing@apwg.org or your organization's security team.

Google Safe Browsing: Report phishing URLs at safebrowsing.google.com/safebrowsing/report_phish

India CERT: Report to incident@cert-in.org.in or via the National Cyber Crime Portal at cybercrime.gov.in

SMS Scams: Forward suspicious texts to 7726 (SPAM) in India or the US.

Platform Features

Complete feature matrix from basic detection to advanced threat intelligence across all attack vectors.

Common Features (All Vectors)

AI Analysis

When you scan a URL, email, or QR code, our Gemini AI analyzes the content like a cybersecurity expert. It checks for phishing patterns, brand impersonation, urgency tactics, and malicious indicators - then provides a confidence score (0-100%) with a detailed explanation of why it's safe or dangerous.

Risk Scoring

Every scan gets a precise 0-100% risk score based on multiple factors: domain age, SSL certificate validity, VirusTotal detections, AI verdict, and sandbox behavior. 0-30% = Safe, 31-70% = Suspicious, 71-100% = Dangerous. This helps you quickly understand the threat level.

History & Caching

When you scan something, we check if we've seen it before. If another user already scanned that URL/QR, you get instant results without waiting. All Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) - like malicious domains and file hashes - are cached for 1 hour to give you faster responses and reduce API calls.

Sandbox Detonation

For suspicious URLs, we open them in an isolated virtual browser (sandbox) and record what happens. We log: page redirects, JavaScript execution, network requests, file downloads, and credential fields. This shows you exactly what a victim would see without putting your system at risk.

SOC Reports

After every scan, you can download a professional PDF incident report with: threat summary, risk score, technical details (headers, redirects, WHOIS), visual evidence (screenshots), and recommended actions. These reports are ready to share with your security team or management.

Threat Intelligence

We check your URLs against 90+ security vendors via VirusTotal and scan web pages via URLScan.io. If a domain is flagged as phishing, malware, or spam by any security engine, you'll know immediately. This gives you real-time threat intelligence from the global security community.

URL Phishing Detection

Web-based phishing link analysis

URL Tracing & Unshortening

When you scan a shortened URL (bit.ly, tinyurl, t.co), we follow all the redirects to find the real destination. This reveals hidden malicious domains that attackers try to mask. You see the full redirect chain and the final landing page URL.

Visual Analysis

We capture a screenshot of the webpage and our AI vision model analyzes it. It detects fake login pages, brand impersonation, suspicious forms, and visual phishing indicators. You get the actual screenshot plus AI's analysis of what makes it dangerous.

Browser Fingerprinting Detection

Malicious sites use Canvas fingerprinting, WebGL tracking, and Font enumeration to identify and track visitors. We detect these techniques and warn you if a site is trying to fingerprint your browser - a common technique in advanced phishing attacks.

Domain Age & SSL Analysis

We check WHOIS records to see when a domain was registered. Phishing domains are often less than 30 days old. We also verify SSL certificates - legitimate sites have valid certs from trusted issuers, while phishing sites often have self-signed or expired certificates.

QR Code (Quishing) Detection

Advanced QR payload analysis & campaign tracking

QR Fingerprinting

Every QR code gets a unique fingerprint based on its visual pattern and payload. If the same QR appears in different attacks, we know it's part of the same campaign. This helps track how widely a phishing attack has spread and identify related incidents.

Threat Actor Attribution

By analyzing the QR structure, payload patterns, and target domains, we can match attacks to known threat groups. For example, if a QR uses specific typosquatting patterns and targets banking sites, it might match TA-QR-002's known TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, Procedures).

Temporal Analysis

When was this QR first seen? When did attacks peak? Are there patterns like "only active on weekdays" or "spikes during lunch hours"? This timing intelligence helps predict when the same attacker might strike again and helps security teams prepare.

Honeytoken Injection

We can embed fake "bait" credentials into QR codes that lead to our controlled servers. If attackers use these credentials, we get an immediate alert - confirming the phishing campaign is active and giving us real-time intelligence on attacker behavior.

Email (EML) Analysis

Forensic email header & attachment analysis

PDF Malware Detection

Attackers often hide malicious JavaScript in PDF attachments that executes when opened. We scan PDFs for: embedded JavaScript, suspicious URLs, file launch actions, and obfuscated content. If a PDF tries to download malware or redirect to phishing sites, we catch it.

Deep Link Extraction

Emails often contain hidden links - in the body, HTML, attachments, even images. We extract every URL, then run each through our full analysis pipeline: VirusTotal checks, sandbox detonation, visual analysis, and AI threat detection. You get a complete link safety report.

IOC Feed Correlation

We check every domain, IP, and URL against multiple threat intel feeds: VirusTotal (90+ engines), URLScan.io, AbuseIPDB, and our own IOC database. If any security vendor has flagged it as malicious, phishing, or suspicious, you'll know immediately with detailed context.

Machine Learning Classification

Our ML model analyzes 20+ features: URL length, domain age, presence of suspicious keywords ("urgent", "verify", "suspend"), typosquatting patterns, and more. It learns from thousands of phishing vs legitimate emails to predict probability with 94%+ accuracy.

SMS (Smishing) Detection

Text message link analysis & shortener unmasking

SMS Link Unmasking

Smishing texts always use URL shorteners to hide malicious domains. We unmask them by following redirects through bit.ly, tinyurl, t.co, and 50+ other shorteners. You see the real destination before clicking - often a fake bank or delivery site designed to steal credentials.

Social Engineering Detection

Scam SMS use psychological triggers: "Your package is stuck", "Urgent: Account will be suspended", "You've won a prize". Our AI detects these urgency patterns, impersonation attempts, and fear-based tactics. It also checks sender ID spoofing and carrier routing.

Voice (Vishing) Detection

Audio analysis for scam call detection

Voice-to-Text Transcription

Upload a scam call recording and we'll convert speech to text using advanced AI. This lets you analyze the exact words scammers use - their scripts, pressure tactics, and impersonation techniques. You can also search for keywords and share evidence with authorities.

Vishing Pattern Recognition

Our AI is trained on thousands of scam call recordings. It recognizes patterns like: "This is your bank calling", "IRS enforcement action", "Tech support refund". It detects urgency creation, authority impersonation, and callback number spoofing - giving you a risk score based on how closely the call matches known vishing scripts.
